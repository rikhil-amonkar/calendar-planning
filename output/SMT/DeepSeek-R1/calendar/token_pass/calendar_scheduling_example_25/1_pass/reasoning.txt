We are given the task to schedule a meeting for Anthony, Pamela, and Zachary on Monday between 9:00 and 17:00.
 The meeting duration is one hour.
 We have the existing schedules and a constraint from Pamela that she does not want to meet after 14:30 (so her availability ends at 14:30).

 Steps:
 1. We will represent time in minutes from 9:00 (which is 0 minutes) to 17:00 (which is 8*60 = 480 minutes).
 2. We define the start time of the meeting as an integer variable in the range [0, 480 - 60] (since the meeting is 60 minutes long).
 3. We define the busy intervals for each participant in minutes (relative to 9:00).

 Anthony's busy intervals (in minutes from 9:00):
  9:30-10:00 -> 30 to 60
  12:00-13:00 -> 180 to 240
  16:00-16:30 -> 420 to 450

 Pamela's busy intervals:

  16:30-17:00 -> 450 to 480
  Additionally, Pamela does not want to meet after 14:30, which is 14:30 - 9:00 = 5 hours and 30 minutes -> 330 minutes.
  So, Pamela is only available until 330 minutes (14:30). Therefore, the meeting must start by 330 - 60 = 270 minutes (so that it ends by 330) but note: the meeting must start and end within her available time? Actually, the constraint is she doesn't want to meet after 14:30, meaning the meeting must end by 14:30. So the meeting must start by 14:30 - 60 minutes = 13:30 (which is 270 minutes) and end by 330 minutes.

 Zachary's busy intervals:

 4. We define constraints for the meeting:
    - The meeting must be one hour (60 minutes) long.
    - The meeting must not overlap with any busy interval for any participant.

 5. We are to find a start time that satisfies all.

 Note: We are only considering Monday.

     For each participant, for each busy interval [busy_start, busy_end], the meeting [start, start+60] does not overlap.
