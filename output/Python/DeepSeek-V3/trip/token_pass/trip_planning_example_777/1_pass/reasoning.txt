Let’s break this down step-by-step before writing the code.

**Constraints summary:**
- Total days: 15
- Cities and required days:
  - Dublin: 5 days
  - Helsinki: 3 days
  - Riga: 3 days
  - Reykjavik: 2 days
  - Vienna: 2 days
  - Tallinn: 5 days
- Special constraints:
  1. Helsinki days must include days 3–5 (for meeting friends).
  2. Vienna must include days 2–3 (annual show).
  3. Tallinn must include days 7–11 (wedding).
- Direct flights (undirected edges):
  - Helsinki–Riga
  - Riga–Tallinn
  - Vienna–Helsinki
  - Riga–Dublin
  - Vienna–Riga
  - Reykjavik–Vienna
  - Helsinki–Dublin
  - Tallinn–Dublin
  - Reykjavik–Helsinki
  - Reykjavik–Dublin
  - Helsinki–Tallinn
  - Vienna–Dublin

**Key observations:**
- Vienna days 2–3 means day 2 and day 3 are in Vienna.
- Helsinki days 3–5 means day 3, 4, 5 in Helsinki? Wait — conflict: Vienna day 3 and Helsinki day 3 can’t both happen unless day 3 is a travel day where you’re in both cities. But the constraint says “meet friends at Helsinki between day 3 and day 5” — that means at least some part of day 3 to day 5 must be in Helsinki. Since Vienna is required day 2–3, maybe day 3 morning in Vienna, fly to Helsinki afternoon, count for both cities.
- Tallinn wedding day 7–11 means days 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 in Tallinn (5 days straight).
- Total days: 5+3+3+2+2+5 = 20, but we only have 15 days. That means some days are counted twice due to travel. Each travel day counts toward both cities. So we need to overlap days to fit 20 “city-days” into 15 calendar days.

We can treat this as a scheduling problem with travel edges.

**Approach:**
We’ll brute-force search over permutations of the 6 cities, assigning consecutive blocks of days (with possible 1-day overlaps for travel), checking:
1. All required day ranges are satisfied.
2. Direct flights exist between consecutive cities.
3. Total calendar days = 15.
4. Each city gets exactly its required days (counting travel days as part of both cities).

We’ll represent schedule as a list of (city, start_day, end_day) where end_day is inclusive, and between segments, end_day of previous = start_day of next (travel day counted for both).

We’ll search for a valid sequence.
