**Step 1 – Understanding the constraints**  
We have 8 cities:  
- Oslo (2 days total, with a required visit on days 16–17)  
- Reykjavik (5 days total, with a required visit between days 9–13)  
- Stockholm (4 days total)  
- Munich (4 days total, with a required visit between days 13–16)  
- Frankfurt (4 days total, with a required visit between days 17–20)  
- Barcelona (3 days total)  
- Bucharest (2 days total)  
- Split (3 days total)  

Total days = 2+5+4+4+4+3+2+3 = 27 days, but we only have 20 days.  
That means some stays overlap because travel days count for both cities.

**Step 2 – Overlap logic**  
If you fly from A to B on day X, you spend day X in both A and B.  
So a 20-day itinerary can "cover" 27 city-days if there are 7 travel days where you are in two cities.

**Step 3 – Fixed date constraints**  
- Days 16–17: Oslo (2 days)  
- Days 17–20: Frankfurt (4 days, starting day 17, so day 17 is in both Oslo and Frankfurt)  
- Days 13–16: Munich (4 days, so days 13,14,15,16 in Munich, but day 16 also in Oslo — possible if you fly Munich→Oslo on day 16 morning or late day 15)  
- Days 9–13: Reykjavik (5 days, so days 9,10,11,12,13 in Reykjavik, but day 13 also in Munich — possible if you fly Reykjavik→Munich on day 13)

**Step 4 – Building a day-by-day plan**  
We need to arrange visits so that travel days align with direct flights.

Let’s start from the fixed points and work backward/forward.

**Step 5 – Direct flights graph**  
We’ll encode the graph in Python and search for a feasible sequence.
