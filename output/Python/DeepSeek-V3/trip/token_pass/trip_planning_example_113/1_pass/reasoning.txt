Let's break down the problem step by step before writing the code.

**Given constraints:**
- Total days: 12
- Naples: 3 days
- Seville: 4 days
- Milan: 7 days
- Direct flights: Milan ↔ Seville, Naples ↔ Milan
- From day 9 to day 12 (inclusive) → must be in Seville for an annual show.

**Important rule:** If you fly on day X from A to B, you spend that day in both cities (counts toward both totals).

---

### Step 1 – Understanding the day counting
If we have:
- Naples: 3 days
- Seville: 4 days
- Milan: 7 days  
Total = 3 + 4 + 7 = 14 days, but we only have 12 calendar days.  
This means there must be **2 overlap days** where we are in two cities (travel days).

So exactly 2 travel days, each travel day counts toward both cities' totals.

---

### Step 2 – Travel possibilities
Direct flights:
1. Milan ↔ Seville  
2. Naples ↔ Milan  
No direct Naples–Seville, so to go Naples ↔ Seville, must go through Milan.

Possible itineraries must start and end somewhere, visiting all three cities.

---

### Step 3 – Seville fixed days constraint
Days 9–12 inclusive = 4 days in Seville.  
That matches exactly the Seville requirement (4 days).  
So Seville visit must be exactly days 9–12, no extra Seville days outside that range.

---

### Step 4 – Placing Naples and Milan
We have days 1–8 and day 12 (end) to place Naples and Milan.

If Seville is days 9–12, then:
- On day 9, we fly into Seville from somewhere (Milan, since only Milan connects to Seville).
- So day 9 is a travel day: morning in Milan, fly to Seville, count for both Milan and Seville.
- Similarly, leaving Seville: day 12 is last day in Seville, but if we leave Seville on day 12 to go somewhere, day 12 counts for both Seville and destination.

---

### Step 5 – Constructing a valid schedule
Let’s try:

We need 7 days Milan, 3 days Naples, 4 days Seville.

Seville: days 9, 10, 11, 12 → 4 days.  
Day 9 is travel from Milan → counts for Milan and Seville.  
So Milan gets +1 from day 9, Seville gets +1 from day 9.

Naples: 3 days. Must be before or after Seville?  
If after Seville: day 12 travel Seville → Milan → Naples? No, can’t go Seville → Naples directly. Must go Seville → Milan (day 12 travel), then Milan → Naples (another travel day). That’s too many travel days maybe.

Better: Naples before Seville.

---

Let’s attempt:

Days 1–3: Naples (3 days)  
Day 4: Travel Naples → Milan (counts for Naples and Milan)  
Days 5–8: Milan (4 more days)  
Day 9: Travel Milan → Seville (counts for Milan and Seville)  
Days 10–12: Seville (3 more days, total Seville = day 9,10,11,12 = 4 days)

Check counts:  
Naples: days 1,2,3,4 = 4 days? Wait, day 4 is travel Naples→Milan, so Naples gets day 4, Milan gets day 4. That’s 4 days Naples, but we need 3. So too many.

So adjust: Naples only 2 full days + 1 travel day from Naples to Milan.

---

### Step 6 – Exact allocation
We need exactly 3 Naples days: could be 2 full + 1 travel, or 3 full + 0 travel?  
If 3 full Naples days, then no travel day counting for Naples, so Naples total = 3. Then travel day Naples→Milan would be extra Naples day, making it 4, so not allowed.  
So Naples must have exactly 1 travel day that counts for it.

Similarly, Milan must have 2 travel days that count for it (since 7 days Milan total, with 12 total days and other cities fixed).

Seville has 1 travel day that counts for it (day 9).

---

Let’s assign:

Day 1–2: Naples (2 days)  
Day 3: Travel Naples → Milan (counts Naples and Milan) → Naples total = 3, Milan total = 1  
Days 4–8: Milan (5 days) → Milan total = 1 + 5 = 6  
Day 9: Travel Milan → Seville (counts Milan and Seville) → Milan total = 7, Seville total = 1  
Days 10–12: Seville (3 days) → Seville total = 1 + 3 = 4

Perfect.

---

### Step 7 – Convert to itinerary format
Day ranges:
- Naples: Day 1–3 (day 3 is travel, but counts for Naples)
- Milan: Day 3–9 (day 9 is travel, but counts for Milan)
- Seville: Day 9–12

Wait, careful: On day 9, morning in Milan, fly to Seville, so day 9 counts for both. In itinerary display, we usually put the arrival city for the full day range? The problem’s example shows day ranges are contiguous per place, so we split at travel days.

Better:
- Naples: Day 1–2 (full days) + Day 3 (travel) — but in range format: Day 1–3 (since day 3 counts for Naples).
- Milan: Day 3–9 (since day 3 arrival, day 9 departure, both count for Milan).
- Seville: Day 9–12.

But day 9 appears in both Milan and Seville ranges in this counting. In output, we should list consecutive stays without overlapping day numbers in ranges.

So:
Day 1–3: Naples  
Day 3–9: Milan  
Day 9–12: Seville  

Yes, day 3 and day 9 are transition days appearing in two ranges.
