Let's break down the problem first.

**Constraints:**
- Total days: 7
- Days in Madrid: 4
- Days in Dublin: 3
- Days in Tallinn: 2
- Must be in Tallinn on day 6 and day 7 (workshop days)
- Direct flights only: Madrid ↔ Dublin, Dublin ↔ Tallinn (no Madrid ↔ Tallinn direct)
- If you fly on day X, you are in both cities on day X (counts toward both totals)

**Key observations:**
- Tallinn days must include day 6 and 7 → so Tallinn days are consecutive days 6–7 (2 days).
- Since Tallinn connects only to Dublin, you must be in Dublin before Tallinn.
- Madrid connects only to Dublin, so to go Madrid ↔ Tallinn, you must go through Dublin.

**Possible sequence logic:**
We need 4 Madrid days, 3 Dublin days, 2 Tallinn days = total 9 city-days, but only 7 calendar days → overlaps happen on travel days.

If you travel from A to B on day X, that day counts for both A and B totals. So we can "double-count" some days to fit 9 city-days into 7 calendar days.

Let’s denote:
- M = Madrid
- D = Dublin
- T = Tallinn

We know T is days 6–7. So day 6: travel from D to T (counts for D and T), day 7: in T only.

We need 4 M days, 3 D days, 2 T days.

Let’s try to build from start:

Day 1: Start somewhere. Likely Madrid (since most days there).
We need to end in Tallinn on day 6–7.

Let’s test a schedule:

Day 1: M  
Day 2: M  
Day 3: M  
Day 4: M → D (travel day: counts for M and D)  
Day 5: D  
Day 6: D → T (travel day: counts for D and T)  
Day 7: T  

Count days:  
M: days 1,2,3,4 = 4 ✅  
D: days 4,5,6 = 3 ✅  
T: days 6,7 = 2 ✅  
Total calendar days: 7 ✅  
Tallinn on days 6–7 ✅  
Direct flights: M–D on day 4, D–T on day 6 ✅  

This works.
