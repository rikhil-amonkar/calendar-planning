**Step 1 – Understanding the problem**  
We start in Richmond District at 9:00 AM.  
We have two friends with availability:  
- Carol: Marina District, 11:30–15:00, needs 60 minutes together.  
- Jessica: Pacific Heights, 15:30–16:45, needs 45 minutes together.  

Travel times are given between districts.  
We want to meet as many friends as possible (here, both if possible) while respecting travel times and meeting duration constraints.

**Step 2 – Possible meeting orders**  
We can meet Carol first, then Jessica, or Jessica first, then Carol.  
But Jessica’s window is later in the day, so meeting Carol first makes more sense because Carol’s window ends at 15:00, and Jessica’s starts at 15:30.

**Step 3 – Time calculations**  
Let’s try meeting Carol first, then Jessica.

1. Start at Richmond at 9:00.  
   Travel to Marina: 9 minutes (given Richmond → Marina = 9).

   Meet Carol for 60 minutes → end at 12:30.

2. Travel from Marina to Pacific Heights: 7 minutes (given Marina → Pacific Heights = 7).  
   Leave Marina at 12:30, arrive Pacific Heights at 12:37.  
   Jessica is available only from 15:30, so we wait until 15:30 to start meeting.  
   Meet Jessica for 45 minutes → end at 16:15 (within Jessica’s window 15:30–16:45).  

This works and meets both friends.

Start Richmond at 9:00 → Pacific Heights: 10 minutes (Richmond → Pacific Heights = 10).  
Arrive 9:10, but Jessica is only available from 15:30, so huge wait. Then after meeting Jessica 15:30–16:15, travel to Marina: Pacific Heights → Marina = 6 minutes, arrive 16:21, but Carol’s window ends at 15:00, so impossible.  
So only Carol-first order works.

**Step 5 – Verify no tighter scheduling possible**  
We could try to shorten wait times by adjusting meeting start times, but Carol must start at or after 11:30, Jessica at or after 15:30, and travel times fixed.  
Our schedule:  
- 9:00–9:09 travel to Marina  
- 9:09–11:30 wait  
- 11:30–12:30 meet Carol  
- 12:30–12:37 travel to Pacific Heights  
- 12:37–15:30 wait  
- 15:30–16:15 meet Jessica  

This meets both friends, so optimal.

**Step 6 – Write program**  
We’ll encode travel times, constraints, then brute-force possible start times in discrete steps to find a schedule that meets both, but since we already reasoned it out, we can directly compute.

But to make the program algorithmic, we can:
1. Represent time in minutes from midnight.
2. Try both orders.
3. For each order, compute earliest arrival at first friend’s location, then schedule meeting as soon as possible within their window respecting duration, then travel to next, etc.
4. Pick order that meets both.
