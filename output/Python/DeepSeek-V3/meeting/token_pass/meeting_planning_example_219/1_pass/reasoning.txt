## **Step 1 — Understanding the problem**

We start at **The Castro at 9:00 AM**.  
We have three friends:

1. **Emily** — Alamo Square — available 11:45–15:15 — needs **105 minutes** (1h45m) with us.
2. **Barbara** — Union Square — available 16:45–18:15 — needs **60 minutes** (1h).
3. **William** — Chinatown — available 17:15–19:00 — needs **105 minutes** (1h45m).

Travel times are symmetric except for a few small differences (e.g., Castro to Chinatown is 20, Chinatown to Castro is 22).

We want to meet as many as possible, but meeting all three may be impossible due to time constraints and travel.

---

## **Step 2 — Feasibility check**

Let’s see if we can meet all three:

- Emily’s meeting: 105 minutes = 1h45m.  
  If we start at 11:45 (her earliest), we finish at 13:30.  
  Then we must travel to the next person.

- Barbara is available 16:45–18:15.  
  William is available 17:15–19:00.

If we meet Emily 11:45–13:30, we have from 13:30 until 16:45 (Barbara’s start) = 3h15m gap.  
We could meet William before Barbara, but William starts only at 17:15, so that’s too late if we want to meet Barbara first.

Let’s check possible orders:

## **Step 3 — Try to fit all three with adjusted meeting times**

We can shorten a meeting if it’s less than their “like to meet” minimum, but the problem says “You’d like to meet Emily for a minimum of 105 minutes” — this sounds like a preference, not a hard constraint.  
But to maximize, we try to meet all three with possibly shorter meetings.

Let’s attempt:

Emily: 11:45–13:00 (75 min)  
Travel to Union: 14 min → arrive 13:14  
Wait until Barbara available 16:45 (long wait)  
Barbara: 16:45–17:15 (30 min)  
Travel to Chinatown: 7 min → arrive 17:22  
William: 17:22–18:22 (60 min)  
William ends 18:22 within his window (ends 19:00).

This meets all three but with shorter times.  
But maybe we can adjust Emily earlier? She starts at 11:45 fixed.

Actually, we can shift Emily earlier within her window? No, she’s available 11:45–15:15, so earliest start 11:45.

We could meet Emily 11:45–13:15 (90 min),  
then go to Union (14 min) → arrive 13:29,  
wait,  
Barbara 16:45–17:30 (45 min),  
travel to Chinatown 7 min → arrive 17:37,  
William 17:37–18:37 (60 min).

Still all three with reduced times.

But the problem says “optimize your goals” — likely means maximize total meeting time or number of friends.  
If meeting all three with reduced times is possible, that’s better than meeting two with full times.

But maybe the “minimum” is a requirement. If it’s a requirement, then we can’t meet all three.  
Let’s assume it’s a requirement (otherwise trivial).

Then possible schedules meeting minimums:

1. Emily + Barbara:  
   Emily 11:45–13:30 (105 min)  
   Travel to Union 14 min → arrive 13:44  
   Wait until 16:45  
   Barbara 16:45–17:45 (60 min)  
   Done.

2. Emily + William:  
   Emily 11:45–13:30  
   Travel to Chinatown 16 min → arrive 13:46  
   Wait until 17:15  
   William 17:15–19:00 (105 min)

3. Barbara + William:  
   Start at Castro 9:00  
   Go to Union earlier (19 min) → arrive 9:19, wait until 16:45 (impossibly long wait, but possible)  
   Barbara 16:45–17:45  
   Travel to Chinatown 7 min → arrive 17:52  
   William 17:52–19:00 → only 68 min, less than 105 min requirement → fails.

So only #1 and #2 meet two friends with their minimums.

Which is better?  
#1: Emily 105 + Barbara 60 = 165 min total meeting.  
#2: Emily 105 + William 105 = 210 min total meeting.

So Emily + William gives more total meeting time.

## **Step 4 — Choose best**

Thus best is:  
Start Castro 9:00,  
go to Alamo Square (8 min) → arrive 9:08, wait until 11:45,  
meet Emily 11:45–13:30,  
travel to Chinatown 16 min → arrive 13:46, wait until 17:15,  
meet William 17:15–19:00.
