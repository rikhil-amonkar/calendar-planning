Let’s break this down step by step before writing the code.

**Step 1 – Understanding the problem**  
We start at **Haight-Ashbury at 9:00 AM**.  
We have 5 friends, each with:  
- A location  
- Availability window  
- Minimum meeting duration desired  
- Travel times between all locations (not symmetric in all cases)  

Goal: Maximize number of friends met, respecting travel times and availability.

**Step 2 – Prioritization by constraints**  
We can’t meet everyone because of time conflicts and travel. Let’s check feasibilities:

1. **Richard** – Pacific Heights, 7:15–10:15 AM, needs 75 min.  
   We start at 9:00 at Haight-Ashbury. Travel to Pacific Heights = 12 min (from table: Haight-Ashbury to Pacific Heights = 12). Arrive 9:12.  
   Latest we can start meeting Richard = 10:15 − 75 min = 9:00.  
   But we arrive at 9:12, so we’re late. However, maybe he can wait? No, 9:12 > 9:00, so we can’t meet for full 75 min. Could meet for less? Problem says “You’d like to meet for minimum of X minutes” — treat as hard requirement? Usually in these puzzles, it’s a requirement. So Richard impossible? Let’s check:  
   If we leave Haight-Ashbury immediately at 9:00, travel 12 min, arrive 9:12, meet until 10:15 (63 min) → less than 75 → fails. So Richard is impossible.

2. **Stephanie** – Mission District, 8:15–13:45, needs 90 min.  
   From Haight-Ashbury at 9:00, travel = 11 min (Haight-Ashbury to Mission District = 11). Arrive 9:11.  
   She’s available until 13:45. Latest start = 13:45 − 90 min = 12:15.  
   So we can start meeting her any time between 9:11 and 12:15 for 90 min. Possible.

3. **Jason** – Fisherman’s Wharf, 8:30–17:45, needs 60 min.  
   Travel times vary depending on where we come from.

4. **Brian** – Russian Hill, 12:15–16:00, needs 120 min.  
   Latest start = 16:00 − 120 min = 14:00. Must start between 12:15 and 14:00.

5. **Sandra** – Bayview, 13:00–19:30, needs 15 min.  
   Latest start = 19:30 − 15 min = 19:15. Easy.
