Let’s break this down step by step before writing the code.

**Step 1 – Understanding the problem**  
We start at Presidio at 9:00 AM.  
We have 9 friends, each with:
- A location
- A time window they are available
- A minimum meeting duration we want
- Travel times between all locations (including Presidio)

Goal: Maximize the number of friends met (or total meeting time? The problem says “meet as many friends as possible,” so primary objective is count, not duration).

**Step 2 – Constraints**  
We cannot meet someone outside their window.  
We must account for travel time between meetings.  
We can meet them for *at least* the minimum duration, possibly more if time allows, but meeting longer reduces chance to meet others.

**Step 3 – Approach**  
This is like a scheduling problem with travel times.  
We can brute-force search permutations of meeting order, but 9! is large (362880). We can prune by feasibility.  
Better: treat as a search over time, choosing next meeting greedily? But greedy may not be optimal for count.  
We’ll try a DFS with backtracking:  
- State: current location, current time, set of people already met  
- Try to schedule each unmet person if feasible:  
  1. Travel from current location to their location (travel time)  
  2. Start time = max(arrival time, their available start)  
  3. If start time + min duration ≤ their available end, we can meet  
  4. Choose to meet for exactly min duration (to save time for others)  
- Keep track of best solution by number met, then by total meeting time.

**Step 4 – Time representation**  
Use minutes since midnight for calculations, convert to "H:MM" for output.

**Step 5 – Travel matrix**  
We’ll store travel times in a dict: `travel[(loc1, loc2)]`.

**Step 6 – Implementation plan**  
1. Parse all friend data into list of dicts.  
2. DFS from start (Presidio, 9:00 AM).  
3. At each step, try all unvisited friends, check feasibility, schedule, recurse.  
4. Keep best itinerary (most people met, tie-break by total meeting time).  
5. Output as JSON.
