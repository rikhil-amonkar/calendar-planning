## **Step 1 – Understanding the problem**
We start at **North Beach at 9:00 AM**.  
We have two friends:

1. **Karen**  
   - Location: Pacific Heights  
   - Available: 18:45 to 20:15 (6:45 PM to 8:15 PM)  
   - Want to meet for **at least 90 minutes** (1.5 hours)  
   - That means we must be with her from *some start time* to *start + 90 min*, entirely within 18:45–20:15.

2. **Mark**  
   - Location: Embarcadero  
   - Available: 13:00 to 17:45 (1:00 PM to 5:45 PM)  
   - Want to meet for **at least 120 minutes** (2 hours)  
   - Entire meeting within 13:00–17:45.

## **Step 3 – Logical constraints**
We can meet them in either order, but must account for travel between locations.

Let’s define:

- **Meeting Mark**: 2 hours (120 min) at Embarcadero between 13:00–17:45.
- **Meeting Karen**: 1.5 hours (90 min) at Pacific Heights between 18:45–20:15.

We must end Karen’s meeting by 20:15, so her meeting can start at 18:45 (end 20:15) or earlier if possible, but she’s only available starting 18:45. So her meeting must be **18:45–20:15** exactly to get the full 90 minutes.

Thus:  
Karen’s meeting is fixed: **18:45 to 20:15** at Pacific Heights.

---

## **Step 4 – Travel to Karen**
We must be at Pacific Heights by 18:45.  
Where are we before that? Either at North Beach or Embarcadero (after meeting Mark).

Travel times to Pacific Heights:
- From North Beach: 8 min
- From Embarcadero: 11 min

So we must leave previous location early enough to arrive at Pacific Heights by 18:45.

---

## **Step 5 – Meeting Mark possibilities**
Mark’s window: 13:00–17:45, need 120 min contiguous.

Possible meeting with Mark:
- Start at 13:00 → end 15:00 (2 hours)
- Start at 13:30 → end 15:30
- Start at 14:00 → end 16:00
- Start at 15:00 → end 17:00
- Start at 15:45 → end 17:45 (exactly)

We must also travel to Karen after Mark (or before Mark if we do Karen first — but Karen is late, so likely Mark first).

## **Step 6 – Try Mark first, then Karen**
We start at North Beach 9:00. We can go to Embarcadero to meet Mark (travel 6 min).  
We can arrive at Embarcadero earliest 9:06, but Mark isn’t available until 13:00. So we’d have a long gap. But we can fill it by staying at North Beach or exploring, but the schedule only cares about meetings, so idle time is fine.

Let’s pick Mark’s meeting: **15:00–17:00** (2 hours).  
Why? Because then we finish at 17:00, travel to Pacific Heights for Karen at 18:45.

Travel from Embarcadero to Pacific Heights = 11 min.  
If we leave Embarcadero at 17:00, we arrive Pacific Heights at 17:11.  
But Karen is available only at 18:45, so we wait 1 hour 34 minutes. That’s fine.

Then meet Karen 18:45–20:15.

## **Step 7 – Check if we can meet Mark earlier and still meet Karen**
If Mark’s meeting is 13:00–15:00, finish 15:00, travel to Pacific Heights (11 min) → arrive 15:11, wait until 18:45 (3.5 hours idle). Still fine.  
But maybe we can go somewhere in between? The problem doesn’t forbid idle time, so either is valid.

We want to “meet as many friends as possible” — here it’s just two, so we meet both. Any schedule that fits both is optimal.

So simplest:  
Mark: 13:00–15:00  
Travel to Pacific Heights: 15:00 + 11 min = 15:11 arrival  
Wait until 18:45  
Karen: 18:45–20:15

## **Step 8 – Verify travel times matrix consistency**
From Embarcadero to Pacific Heights is 11 min, yes.

## **Step 9 – Write program to compute this**
We’ll encode travel times, constraints, and brute-force possible start times for Mark (in 15-min increments) to see all valid schedules, then pick one (e.g., earliest finish with Mark to minimize idle? or latest start with Mark to minimize idle before Karen?).

But since idle time doesn’t matter for meeting count, any valid schedule works. We’ll pick Mark 13:00–15:00 as it’s earliest.
