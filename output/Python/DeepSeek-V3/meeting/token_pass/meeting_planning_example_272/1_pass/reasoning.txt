## **Step 1 – Understanding the problem**
We start at **Russian Hill at 9:00 AM**.  
We have three friends with availability:

1. **Timothy** – Embarcadero, 9:45 AM – 5:45 PM, wants 120 minutes.
2. **Patricia** – Nob Hill, 6:30 PM – 9:45 PM, wants 90 minutes.
3. **Ashley** – Mission District, 8:30 PM – 9:15 PM, wants 45 minutes.

Travel times are given between locations.

We want to meet as many friends as possible, respecting:
- Travel time between meetings
- Minimum meeting durations
- Time windows

---

## **Step 2 – Observations**
- **Timothy** is available all day, so we can meet him first after arriving.
- **Patricia** is only available in the evening.
- **Ashley** is available for a short time in the evening, overlapping with Patricia’s window.
- Travel times are symmetric-ish but not exactly equal.

We need to decide the **order** of meetings to maximize number of friends met.

Possible orders:
1. Timothy → Patricia → Ashley  
2. Timothy → Ashley → Patricia  
3. Patricia → Timothy → Ashley (impossible, Timothy’s window ends before Patricia’s starts)  
4. etc.

But Ashley’s window is very late and short, so meeting Patricia first might make Ashley impossible if travel time is too long.
