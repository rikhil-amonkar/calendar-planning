Let’s break this down step by step before writing the code.  

We have:  
- **Work hours**: 9:00–17:00  
- **Duration**: 1 hour  
- **Day**: Monday  
- **James busy**: 11:30–12:00, 14:30–15:00  
- **John busy**: 9:30–11:00, 11:30–12:00, 12:30–13:30, 14:30–16:30  

We need to find a 1-hour slot where neither is busy, within 9:00–17:00.  

---

### Step 1 – Combine busy times
We can merge busy intervals for each person, then find gaps where both are free.  

**James busy intervals**:  
1. 11:30–12:00  
2. 14:30–15:00  

**John busy intervals**:  
1. 9:30–11:00  
2. 11:30–12:00  
3. 12:30–13:30  
4. 14:30–16:30  

---

### Step 2 – Find common free times
We can convert times to minutes from 9:00 for easier calculation:  
- 9:00 = 0 min  
- 17:00 = 480 min (8 hours later)  

**James busy in minutes**:  
1. 11:30–12:00 → 150–180 min from 9:00  
2. 14:30–15:00 → 330–360 min from 9:00  

**John busy in minutes**:  
1. 9:30–11:00 → 30–120 min  
2. 11:30–12:00 → 150–180 min  
3. 12:30–13:30 → 210–270 min  
4. 14:30–16:30 → 330–450 min  

---

### Step 3 – Merge and find gaps
We can find free slots for each person, then intersect them.  

**James free**:  
- 0–150  
- 180–330  
- 360–480  

**John free**:  
- 0–30  
- 120–150  
- 180–210  
- 270–330  
- (Note: 330–450 busy, 450–480 free)  

Intersect:  
1. 0–30 (both free) → 9:00–9:30 (30 min, too short for 1 hour)  
2. 120–150 (both free) → 11:00–11:30 (30 min, too short)  
3. 180–210 (both free) → 12:00–12:30 (30 min, too short)  
4. 270–330 (both free) → 13:30–14:30 (60 min, works!)  
5. 450–480 (both free) → 16:30–17:00 (30 min, too short)  

So the only 1-hour slot is **13:30–14:30**.  

---

### Step 4 – Check against original schedules
- James: free 13:30–14:30 (yes, only busy 14:30–15:00 starts exactly at 14:30, so 13:30–14:30 is fine)  
- John: free 13:30–14:30 (yes, busy 12:30–13:30 ends at 13:30, and next busy starts 14:30)  

Perfect.
