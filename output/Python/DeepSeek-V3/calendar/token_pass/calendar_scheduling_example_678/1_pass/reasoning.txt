Let’s break this down step by step before writing the code.  

**Given:**  
- Work hours: 9:00–17:00 (inclusive start, exclusive end for slots).  
- Days considered: Monday, Tuesday.  
- Meeting duration: 1 hour.  
- Russell’s busy:  
  - Monday: 10:30–11:00  
  - Tuesday: 13:00–13:30  
- Alexander’s busy:  
  - Monday: 9:00–11:30, 12:00–14:30, 15:00–17:00  
  - Tuesday: 9:00–10:00, 13:00–14:00, 15:00–15:30, 16:00–16:30  
- Russell prefers not to meet on Tuesday before 13:30.  

**Step 1 – Find free slots for each person on each day within work hours.**  
We can represent time in minutes from 9:00 = 0, 17:00 = 480.  

**Step 2 – Intersect free slots for both people on each day.**  

**Step 3 – Apply Russell’s Tuesday preference (no meeting before 13:30 on Tuesday).**  

**Step 4 – Find a 1-hour slot in the intersected free times.**  

**Step 5 – Output day and time range in HH:MM:HH:MM format.**  

---

**Let’s compute manually first to verify there’s a solution:**  

**Monday:**  
Russell free: 9:00–10:30, 11:00–17:00  
Alexander free: 11:30–12:00, 14:30–15:00  
Intersection:  
- 11:30–12:00 (30 min) → too short for 1 hour.  
- 14:30–15:00 (30 min) → too short.  
So Monday has no 1-hour slot.  

**Tuesday:**  
Russell free: 9:00–13:00, 13:30–17:00  
Alexander free: 10:00–13:00, 14:00–15:00, 15:30–16:00, 16:30–17:00  
Intersection:  
- 10:00–13:00 (Russell free, Alexander free) → but Russell prefers not before 13:30 on Tuesday, so discard before 13:30.  
  Actually, 10:00–13:00 is partly before 13:30, but the part 10:00–13:00 is entirely before 13:30, so Russell’s preference removes all of it.  
- 14:00–15:00 (1 hour) → Russell free (13:30–17:00 includes this), Alexander free 14:00–15:00.  
  This works: 14:00–15:00 Tuesday.  

So solution: **Tuesday 14:00–15:00**.
