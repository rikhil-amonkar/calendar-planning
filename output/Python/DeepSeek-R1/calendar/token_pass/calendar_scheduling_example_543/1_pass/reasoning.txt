We are given the work hours from 9:00 to 17:00 (5 PM) on Monday.
 We are to schedule a one-hour meeting for James and John.

 Existing schedules:
 James: busy on Monday during 11:30-12:00 and 14:30-15:00.
 John: busy on Monday during 9:30-11:00, 11:30-12:00, 12:30-13:30, 14:30-16:30.

 We note that the meeting must be within 9:00 to 17:00.

 Steps:
 1. Represent the available time for each person by inverting their busy intervals within the work hours.
 2. Find the intersection of available times for both James and John.
 3. Look for a one-hour slot in the intersection.

 Since the work hours are 9:00 to 17:00, we can consider the entire day from 9:00 to 17:00 and subtract the busy times.

 Let's break down the available times for each:

 James:
   Busy: [11:30-12:00], [14:30-15:00]
   Available (within 9:00-17:00):
     [9:00-11:30], [12:00-14:30], [15:00-17:00]

 John:
   Busy: [9:30-11:00], [11:30-12:00], [12:30-13:30], [14:30-16:30]
   Available:
     [9:00-9:30], [11:00-11:30], [12:00-12:30], [13:30-14:30], [16:30-17:00]

 Now, we need the intersection of James and John's available times.

 Let's list the available intervals for both and then find overlapping intervals:

 James available intervals:
   A: 9:00 to 11:30
   B: 12:00 to 14:30
   C: 15:00 to 17:00

 John available intervals:
   D: 9:00 to 9:30
   E: 11:00 to 11:30
   F: 12:00 to 12:30
   G: 13:30 to 14:30
   H: 16:30 to 17:00

 Now, we check each overlapping interval:

 A (9:00-11:30) and D (9:00-9:30): overlap [9:00-9:30] -> 30 minutes (not enough for 1 hour)
 A and E (11:00-11:30): overlap [11:00-11:30] -> 30 minutes (not enough)

 B (12:00-14:30) and F (12:00-12:30): overlap [12:00-12:30] -> 30 minutes (not enough)
 B and G (13:30-14:30): overlap [13:30-14:30] -> 1 hour (exactly)

 C (15:00-17:00) and H (16:30-17:00): overlap [16:30-17:00] -> 30 minutes (not enough)

 So the only one-hour slot is from 13:30 to 14:30.

 However, note that James is available until 14:30 and John is available from 13:30 to 14:30. 
 The meeting from 13:30 to 14:30 is exactly one hour and falls within both availabilities.

 Therefore, we schedule the meeting on Monday from 13:30 to 14:30.

 Let's code accordingly.

 Approach in code:
 We'll represent time in minutes from start of day (9:00 is 0 minutes, 17:00 is 8*60 = 480 minutes).
 But note: 9:00 to 17:00 is 8 hours = 480 minutes.

 Steps in code:
 1. Define the work hours: start_time = 9*60, end_time = 17*60 (in minutes)
 2. Represent busy intervals for each person in minutes from start of day (9:00 as 0).
    For example:
      James busy: 
        11:30-12:00 -> (11:30-9:00) = (2*60+30) = 150 to (12:00-9:00)=180 minutes.
        14:30-15:00 -> (14:30-9:00)= (5*60+30)=330 to (15:00-9:00)=360 minutes.

 3. Create available intervals for each person by inverting the busy intervals within work hours.

 4. Find the overlapping available intervals between the two people.

 5. Check for a gap of at least the meeting duration (60 minutes).

 6. Return the first available slot.

 Since we know there is a solution, we can break once we find one.
